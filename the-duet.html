Set the partner to 0% and it ignores you. Set it to 100% and it copies you, which turns out to be unsettling. In one study, people synchronised best, and felt most in sync, with a virtual partner that adapted to them only partly (Fairhurst, Janata & Keller, 2013).

Instrument · no conductor needed

Sixty fireflies, sixty rhythms

In the Great Smoky Mountains, thousands of fireflies of one species, Photinus carolinus, flash together on June nights. No firefly leads. Each nudges its own timing toward the flashes it sees. Raise the coupling and watch order appear.

Tipping point
≈ 0.80

This is the Kuramoto model (1975). Each firefly has its own natural speed ω and keeps it. Coupling K pulls each phase θ toward the others:

dθᵢ/dt = ωᵢ + (K/N) · Σⱼ sin(θⱼ − θᵢ)

Below a critical coupling the field stays scattered. Above it, synchrony switches on, even though no two fireflies are the same. For this field the theory puts the tipping point near K ≈ 0.8.

What happens between brains

Understanding looks like synchrony

Speaker and listenerWhen one person tells a story, the listener's brain activity follows the speaker's. Listeners whose brains run slightly ahead understand best.Stephens, Silbert & Hasson, 2010
Tapping pairsTwo people tapping together both adjust to each other at once. Neither simply follows. They become one system of two predictors.Konvalinka et al., 2010
ClassroomsStudents whose brain rhythms were more in step with each other were more engaged in class.Dikker et al., 2017
ChoirsWhen singers breathe and sing together, their heart rates rise and fall together.Vickhoff et al., 2013

Karl Friston and Chris Frith modelled two songbirds as prediction machines, each guessing the other's next note. The birds converged on a shared song, a state mathematicians call generalised synchrony, while staying two birds. They called the paper A Duet for One.

The point

In step, and still two

The fireflies keep their own rhythms and still flash as one. The tapping partner works best when it bends partway, not all the way. The birds share a song without sharing a brain.

That's the shape of connection that adds to each mind instead of absorbing it. Listen closely enough to predict the other person. Then leave room for them to surprise you.

Two predictors, one rhythm, still two.
