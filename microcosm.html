Life at the edges

Polyextremophiles survive several extremes at once. Some of the toughest live in ordinary places: moss, soil, dust.

Tardigrades survive by drying into a "tun," a shrivelled state with metabolism almost at zero, then wake when water returns. One species makes a protein called Dsup that wraps its DNA. Put into human cells in a dish, Dsup cut X-ray damage by about 40 percent (Hashimoto et al., 2016).

Instrument · letters from the sky

A snowflake is a record of its fall

The physicist Ukichiro Nakaya grew the first artificial snow crystals in 1936 and found that their shape is set by temperature and humidity. He wrote that snow crystals are letters sent from the sky. Read one and you know the air it fell through.

Field guide

Go and look

Find a tardigrade

  1. Pick a pinch of dry moss or lichen from a wall, roof or tree trunk.
  2. Soak it in rainwater or bottled water in a shallow dish for a few hours, or overnight.
  3. Squeeze the moss out over the dish and look at the water with a 20–40× stereo microscope, or a clip-on phone macro lens.
  4. Look for slow, plump, eight-legged crawlers clinging to the debris, about half a millimetre long. You will probably also see rotifers and nematodes.

Find stardust

  1. Scoop sediment from a roof gutter, ideally on a flat roof that hasn't been cleaned in years.
  2. Rinse it in water and draw out the magnetic particles with a magnet in a plastic bag.
  3. Sieve for grains of about 0.2–0.4 mm and search them under a microscope.
  4. Look for tiny glossy spheres with stripes or a crystal pattern. Most magnetic spheres are industrial, so true micrometeorites are rare finds. Jon Larsen, a jazz guitarist, found the first urban ones this way (Genge et al., 2017).

Around 5,200 tonnes of extraterrestrial dust reach Earth's surface every year (Rojas et al., 2021). Some of it is on your roof right now. And you are a microcosm too: your body hosts about 38 trillion bacteria, slightly more than the number of your own cells (Sender, Fuchs & Milo, 2016).

The universe isn't only up there. It's in the gutter, waiting for a magnet.
